## Running the room

**Open**
> "This is a formal meeting about your work. [Name] is taking notes. You can have a support person — do you want to get one before we start?"

**Then, before anything else:**
> "Nobody is taking action against you for being sick or for looking after your mum. That's not what this is."

Say it out loud, early. It costs you nothing and it removes his best argument.

**Put each item as a fact, then a question. No adjectives.**
> "On Saturday the fifth, the records show travel started at 5:19 for an eight o'clock job in Chapel Hill. Four seconds on site, checked out at 7:46, job not done. What happened?"

**Then stop talking.** Let the silence sit. Do not fill it.

**On the passenger, ask — don't accuse:**
> "Who's been in the work vehicle during working hours? The vehicle's insured for employees on company business. I need to know where we stand."

**Close without deciding:**
> "I'm not making any decision today. I'll consider what you've told me and you'll hear from me in writing."

## Six things not to do

1. **Don't decide in the room.** Adjourn. Always.
2. **Don't mention the FBT figure, the gift card, or anything about what he's cost you.** It reads as retaliation.
3. **Don't say "disappointed."** Don't make it personal or paternal. You are the employer in that room, not his father.
4. **Don't raise the video, the drug joke, or anything you can't evidence.** It will cost you every solid item you have.
5. **Don't bring up the leave itself.** Notification, yes. Entitlement, never.
6. **Don't let it run past 30 minutes.** Long meetings become arguments.
